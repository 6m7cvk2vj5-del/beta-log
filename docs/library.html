Sloth & Monkey

warmup · control · 3 easy problems, each twice

Climb each problem once as slowly and statically as you can (sloth), then once with big, swingy dynamic movement (monkey). Notice which style feels less natural and spend a little extra time there.

  1. Arms straight, hang off the skeleton and move by turning the hips

Three-finger sloth

warmup · control · 1-2 easy jug problems

Climb slowly using only the middle three fingers on each hold, hands open and extended, arms as straight as you can keep them. Builds an efficient, relaxed grab.

  1. Arms straight, hang off the skeleton and move by turning the hips

Robot drill

warmup · control · technique · 2-3 easy problems

Move one limb at a time. The rest of the body stays completely still until that limb has settled on the next hold or position, then the next limb goes.

  1. Start balanced, all four limbs on holds
  2. Move one hand only. Everything else stays still
  3. Settled? Now one foot only, to the next hold

Silent feet

warmup · footwork · 3-4 easy problems

Place every foot so quietly that nobody could hear it. Make contact slowly with a relaxed ankle and the smallest imprint on the hold.

  1. Hover the toe above the hold for a moment
  2. Place it slowly and quietly, with a relaxed ankle

Glue feet

footwork · control · 3 problems

Once a foot lands on a hold it stays put until the next move makes you shift it. Forces you to place it right the first time.

  1. Hover the toe above the hold for a moment
  2. Place it slowly and quietly, with a relaxed ankle

Hover feet

warmup · footwork · 3 easy problems

Hold your toe just above each foothold for about three seconds, then place it. Builds the habit of landing precisely once instead of fixing it afterwards.

  1. Hover the toe above the hold for a moment
  2. Place it slowly and quietly, with a relaxed ankle

Hover hands

control · warmup · 2-3 easy problems

Hold each hand just above the next hold for about three seconds before taking it. Trains body tension and control between holds.

  1. Hold the hand just above the next hold for about three seconds
  2. Then take it, keeping the body tight and still

One touch

control · footwork · 3-4 problems below your limit

Each hand and foot touches each hold once. No readjusting a bad placement, and your eyes stay on the next hold until it is latched.

Eyes on feet

footwork · warmup · 3 easy problems

Look at every foothold before you step on it and keep looking for about two seconds after it lands. Fixes stepping without really seeing the hold.

  1. Hover the toe above the hold for a moment
  2. Place it slowly and quietly, with a relaxed ankle

Pause every move

control · 2-3 problems, up and down

Hold a clean, still position for three seconds after each move before reaching for the next hold. Exposes where you rely on momentum.

Straight-arm traverse

control · technique · 2-3 laps of an easy traverse

Traverse with your arms as straight as possible and move by rotating and shifting the hips. Hang off the skeleton, not the biceps.

  1. Arms straight, hang off the skeleton and move by turning the hips

One-hand traverse

control · 2 laps per hand, slab or vertical

Use one hand only, the other hangs at your side. Each time a hand leaves the wall, find a stable body position for a second or two.

  1. Keep one foot off the wall and balance over the other

One-leg climb

control · technique · 3 easy problems, alternate legs

Keep one foot off the wall for the whole problem. Teaches balance and how to manage momentum and weight over a single foot.

  1. Keep one foot off the wall and balance over the other

No-hands slab

footwork · technique · 3-5 min on easy slab

Climb easy slab without using your hands. Hips over feet, smearing and edging only.

Take-away

footwork · play · 1 easy problem, 5-6 rounds

Pick an easy problem with plenty of feet and remove one foothold each time you pass it. Keep going until it is hard but still possible.

Drop-knee laps

technique · 3 problems

On each problem use as many drop-knees as you can: toe on a hip-height foothold, knee rolled in and pointing down, hips turned to the wall.

Flagging laps

technique · warmup · 3 problems

On each problem use as many flags as you can, inside and outside, to counter the swing and keep your balance on small feet.

  1. Both feet on holds, weight balanced
  2. Lift one foot and swing the leg out to counterbalance

Hook laps

technique · 3 problems

Use as many heel hooks and toe hooks as the problem allows. Heel hooks pull the hips in and give reach; toe hooks train the core.

  1. Hook the heel over a hold at about hip height
  2. Pull with the hamstring so the hips come in toward the wall

Backstep traverse

technique · footwork · 2-3 laps

Traverse using backsteps on the outside edge of the shoe so the hip turns into the wall. Straighten the arms and rest in the turned position.

Deadpoint practice

technique · 5-6 moves, just beyond static

Pick moves slightly too far to reach statically. Pull and let the hips drive, and take the hold at the very top of the motion where it weighs least.

Same problem three ways

technique · control · 1 problem, 3 versions

Climb the same easy problem three times, each time starting each move differently: hips first, feet first, then hands first.

Downclimb

control · footwork · 3 easy problems

Climb down what you climb up, ideally with the same quiet feet. Exposes weak footwork because you cannot see the holds as easily.

Add-on (play)

play · warmup · 10 min with a partner

Take turns: the first climber makes three moves, the next copies them and adds one, and so on until someone slips. Then start over.

Copy me (play)

play · warmup · 10 min with a partner

One person sets a line of holds as they move and the other follows exactly the same holds and body positions. Swap after each line.

Training structure

Training structure

How the app organizes training, as it is built today.

Cycle phases

Skill & Stamina

High-volume, mostly submaximal climbing to build technique, movement skill, and aerobic capacity. Keep most climbing well below max difficulty — avoid grinding on near-limit problems this phase.

  • 10-20 min Warmup + mobility
  • 60-120+ min Varied submaximal climbing — high vertical footage, only light-moderate pump
  • Light strength/core
  • Cooldown

Max Strength & Power

Lower-volume, high-intensity: near-limit bouldering and short, powerful efforts.

  • 15-25 min Warmup + mobility
  • 30-60 min Near-limit / hypergravity bouldering
  • 20-30 min Finger-strength work (hangboard)
  • 10-20 min Pull-power work
  • 15-25 min Core
  • 10-40 min Antagonist/stabilizer + posterior chain (fine as a separate day)
  • Cooldown

Power-Endurance

Moderate-high intensity with short rest, building the ability to climb pumped without falling apart.

  • 20-30 min Progressive warmup
  • 30-60 min Interval-style climbing (e.g. 4x4s or similar)
  • 15-20 min Finger strength-endurance
  • 10-20 min Pull-muscle endurance
  • 15-25 min Core
  • 20-40 min Antagonist/posterior chain (fine as a separate day)
  • Cooldown

Taper

Sharp drop in volume so the previous weeks' adaptations show up. Short sessions (20-40 min), one or two brief high-intensity touches early in the week, full rest days by the end. No new fatigue.

  • Climbing + a little finger maintenance (a couple easy hangs, not a hangboard session) — early in the week
  • Strength, antagonist, core — skippable this week, not just reduced

Warmup skeleton

Small to big, the same every session, only the exercises inside it rotate:

  1. Blood flow (cardio): joint circles always, plus one rotating item.
  2. Prehab (antagonist): 3 to 4 light exercises at 1x5 to 1x10, from body regions not yet covered this week.
  3. Movement pattern (mobility): one coordinated compound movement, e.g. world's greatest stretch.
  4. Finger activation (fingers): last, only when the session includes climbing or fingers.

One play item is included somewhere in the warmup, and some prehab is saved for the cooldown.

Climbing session model

A climbing session is built from these components, in this order:

  1. Warmup: blood flow, prehab, movement pattern, then finger activation last
  2. Technique drills, carried inside the easy climbing
  3. Main climbing: max effort in Max Strength & Power, volume in Skill & Stamina, intervals in Power-Endurance
  4. Fingers, after climbing: heavy hangs only on a heavy-fingers day, otherwise a short finger-health circuit
  5. Strength, antagonist and core work
  6. Cooldown with hip mobility

What stays at each length

Cuts come from the bottom of the list. The warmup (with finger activation), the main climbing and the cooldown are never cut.

Heavy-fingers day

No limit or max-effort climbing. Warmup, technique-only climbing at submaximal effort, then heavy finger training, then cooldown. In the Max Strength & Power phase there is at least one of these per week; the app suggests it until one is logged.

Weekly rules

  • You set the least number of climbing sessions per week (default 2).
  • Missed components this week (technique, max effort, fingers, strength, antagonist/prehab, mobility) are added to the next session within its time budget, at most two extras, in priority order.
  • If the log suggests this is the only session of the week (nothing logged yet and few days left, or recent weeks have had one climb), nothing is stacked on: climbing and health only. You can override this on the Ask screen.
  • If you are behind on the weekly minimum with few days left, climbing is favoured over exercise-only sessions.

Profile (four areas)

  • Strength: fingers, pulling, power, endurance
  • Mobility: range of motion and tension in hips and shoulders
  • Coordination: footwork, precision, balance and timing
  • Awareness: attention, nerve, reading the sequence, breathing

Each check-in is six comparisons (one per pair of areas): you pick which statement holds you back more. Example statements:

Strength

  • My fingers or forearms give out before I run out of ideas.
  • I can see the move but I can't pull or hold it.
  • I run out of power on the hardest moves.
  • I pump out on climbs that should feel easy.

Mobility

  • I can't get my hips close to the wall or my foot high enough.
  • Stiff hips or shoulders stop me getting into the position I want.
  • I avoid moves that need a big stretch or a high step.
  • The same spots feel tight after a session.

Coordination

  • My feet are sloppy on small holds.
  • I lose my balance mid-move and swing off.
  • I mistime dynamic moves.
  • I place a foot, then have to readjust it.

Awareness

  • I tense up or hesitate at the crux.
  • I lose the sequence or miss holds I could use.
  • I climb on autopilot without noticing what my body is doing.
  • I hold my breath or grip harder when it gets tough.
